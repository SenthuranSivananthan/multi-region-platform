Secrets and certificates: two patterns

Secrets and certificates can be loaded into any vault by a pipeline. That makes a vault in each region practical.

1Standard or Premium vault in each region
Created in
An approved pipeline loads secrets and certificates into each regional vault. IaC configures the vaults.
Outage
Each region keeps full read and write on its own vault.
Best for
Mission-critical workloads that need a short recovery time.
Watch for
You must keep the vaults in sync with your pipeline.
2Standard or Premium vault in a paired region
Created in
Pipeline populates primary; Microsoft asynchronously replicates to its paired-region copy.
Outage
Read-only until failover ends: you can read, but not create, update, or rotate.
Best for
Less critical workloads that accept read-only access and Microsoft's failover timing.
Watch for
Check eligible same-geography pairing and exclusions. Microsoft can delay failover for hours or longer.

Design guidance

Treat each region as a deployment stamp. Roll changes out sequentially. Never store secret values in IaC. Microsoft-managed failover keeps the vault URI; retry failed requests. Read-only permits supported cryptography and backup, not writes or policy changes. App-level fallback needs ready identities, connectivity, and versions. Failover and return-to-primary checklist.

Managed HSM and EKM do not hold secrets or certificates. Use Standard or Premium vaults for both.
